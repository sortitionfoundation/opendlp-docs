title: Basic data
description: Setting up for a successful process

Before you start working in earnest on the democratic lottery, you need to have established some basic data about the deliberative process that you are organising:

- **Name of the assembly**. E.g. Sortonia Citizens' Jury on Health, Sortonia Climate Assembly.

- **The question** under consideration -- this should be short and snappy. Think of it as a news headline - it doesn't tell the whole story, but invites you to read more. E.g. What would help people across Sortonia live healthier, better lives?

- **The remit** which explains a bit more of what the assembly will be deliberating on. This should be a couple of sentences. The aim is to spark interest so that people register.

- **Accountability**. One of the key reasons people don't register is because they don't think decision-makers will act on the recommendations. Before you start recruiting participants you need to be sure that the recommendations they make will be effective -- this requires engagement with key stakeholders and the holders of political power in the area.

- **Size**. How many people will be in the assembly? 

- **Dates, times and venue**. You are going to be asking people to register their interest in participation. They'll need to be able to check that they are available, and, in the case of in-person events, they'll need to know that the venue is accessible. When you set dates, make sure you think carefully about holidays and religious festivals to give as many people as possible a chance to be included: for instance, meetings on a Friday evening or during Saturday may exclude Jewish people from participating.

- **Remuneration.** It is important that people are compensated for their participation -- otherwise you effectively exclude anyone who is unable to donate their time for free. What is more, the more you pay, the more likely it is that people sign up.
